The article looks back at Salesforce CPQ, a quote-to-cash product that Salesforce bought as SteelBrick. It sets out the key dates: the bootstrapped startup, the $360M acquisition announced in December 2015, the rebrand to Salesforce CPQ, the March 2025 End of Sale announcement, and the June 2026 certification retirements. The Salesforce CPQ Admin exam closed on August 31, 2026 and officially retires on February 1, 2027.

The author notes that CPQ is a managed package with an SBQQ data model that the ecosystem has long considered complex, with pricing rule stacks, degrading quote line performance and fragile customizations. End of Sale means no new customers, while existing customers keep support and renewals. Salesforce's investment has moved to Revenue Cloud, so CPQ is no longer the direction of the platform, though it is not yet end of life.

The piece is largely reflective, drawing on Reddit reactions that mourn the loss of the CPQ specialism rather than the product itself. The author uses the episode to argue that specialist skills tend to fold into what follows when Salesforce reinvents a product, citing Classic and Workflow Rules and Process Builder as earlier examples. The closing advice is to expect the ground to shift rather than to avoid specializing.
