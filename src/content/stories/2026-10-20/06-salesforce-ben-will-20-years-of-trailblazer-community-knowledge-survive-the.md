Salesforce has announced that the online Trailblazer Community forums are moving to Slack, with the new experience becoming generally available in November 2026. The forums become read-only on October 30, 2026, so members can still browse and search existing posts but cannot publish new ones, and there is a gap before the Slack community opens during which new questions cannot be asked. The move is presented as a response to member requests for real-time conversation, easier peer connection and quicker access to trusted answers. The Slack version will be built around channels chosen during onboarding based on location, role, industry and products, a Slackbot to help find channels and Trailhead resources, and an Answers Agent AI that draws on trusted community knowledge. Joining is free, requires no paid Slack license, and uses an existing Trailblazer ID.

The historical forum archive is to remain accessible and searchable after October 30, so members need not export their saved threads. New answers in Slack will not be immediately visible to web searches. Accepted answers from product channels are to be anonymized, summarized and synced back to the website starting in early 2027. The author flags several gaps: answers posted before that rollout may not be indexed, only accepted product-channel answers will sync, full conversations will not be preserved, and contributors will receive less public credit. Local and specialist Trailblazer groups will not get their own Slack spaces by default. Instead, they will sit within regional hubs and global channels, group pages will stay on the separate group site, and new public channel requests will be reviewed by Salesforce.

The article gives practical advice for preparing: check which email address is linked to your Trailblazer ID and consider using a personal address, find out whether your employer blocks Slack and, if so, plan to use a personal device, and learn the basics via the Get Started in Slack trail on Trailhead. The author is cautious about whether the AI agent could reduce human participation and reputation-building, and notes that the FAQ leaves open questions such as whether the agent will use the forum archive, how reputation carries over, and what happens to the Forum Ambassador program. The author concludes that the success of the move depends on balancing Slack's immediacy with the lasting, searchable value of forums, and encourages members to post outstanding questions before October 30 and to keep helping others once the new community launches.
