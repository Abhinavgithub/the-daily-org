The article introduces Model Context Protocol servers as a standardized interface for linking artificial intelligence hosts to enterprise systems like Salesforce. It outlines a three-part architecture comprising a host application, a client, and a server that securely exposes tools and resources. This design aims to replace custom integrations by allowing large language models to automatically discover and execute connected capabilities.

Practical examples demonstrate how sales and support teams can retrieve pipeline data or automate cross-platform workflows using simple natural language commands. These scenarios eliminate the need to manually switch between separate applications and navigate multiple forms to complete routine tasks.

Financial and governance impacts are also addressed, noting that agent-driven calls will begin consuming standard flex credits in November. The piece explains the introduction of Agentic Identity to distinguish between standard user operations and scoped agent permissions. Testing environments including Sandboxes and Scratch Orgs remain exempt from these billing changes, allowing teams to evaluate the technology safely before production deployment.
