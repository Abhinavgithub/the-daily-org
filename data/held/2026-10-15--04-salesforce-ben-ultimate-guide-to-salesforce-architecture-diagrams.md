Architecture diagrams serve as visual contracts that align stakeholders and reduce communication failures during design and delivery. Salesforce recently refreshed its Reference Diagrams and Lucidchart shape libraries to incorporate Agentforce components, update product naming like Data 360, and improve accessibility standards. These changes ensure that architectural representations stay synchronized with the evolving platform without requiring teams to abandon existing frameworks.

The guide outlines five core diagram types tailored to different communication goals. Capability maps establish business requirements without exposing technical implementation. Roadmaps visualize phased delivery and component dependencies over time. System landscapes document the broader ecosystem of connected products and external systems. Solution architectures detail technical integrations, authentication methods, and data movement patterns. Interaction process flows sequence events across agents, automated processes, and human handoffs.

Practitioners are advised to match each diagram type to the intended audience rather than attempting to consolidate everything into a single view. Business leaders typically require capability maps and roadmaps, whereas engineering teams need solution architectures and interaction flows. The article stresses that using multiple complementary diagrams maintains a consistent narrative throughout the project lifecycle.
